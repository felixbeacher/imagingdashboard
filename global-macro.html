From macro signals to imaging decisions

Here is how the economic picture could affect choices about imaging equipment and services. These are questions to investigate, not proven predictions.

Capital budgets and scanner purchases

Signals to check Economic growth, borrowing rates and inflation.

Possible implication Costlier borrowing or tighter budgets may delay equipment replacement or expansion.

Next check Local capital budgets, lease terms, procurement approvals and scanner age.

Equipment delivery and maintenance

Signals to check Supply-chain pressure and geopolitical events.

Possible implication Delays or higher component costs may affect installation and service.

Next check Vendor delivery commitments, parts coverage and maintenance contracts.

Imaging service operating costs

Signals to check Local energy, staffing and general inflation.

Possible implication Rising running costs can erode the value of a flat hospital budget.

Next check Local utility tariffs, staffing budgets, equipment uptime and contract indexation.

Share-market valuations

An optional investor perspective: the traditional US Buffett Indicator compares US corporate equity market value with US GDP, not the value of a scanner or an individual imaging company.

US share valuation — Buffett Indicator

Do US share prices look high or low?

The US Buffett Indicator (corporate equities to trailing 4-quarter US GDP) is 262.1% in 2026 Q2, compared with a typical past level of 130.7% across 30 earlier years of data. It is at the 100th percentile of the available history. This makes US shares look expensive compared with the past, but does not prove they are overpriced.

A cautious view of financial markets (Austrian School)

This viewpoint asks whether cheap borrowing has pushed share prices too high or encouraged projects that may struggle when interest rates rise. For imaging businesses, check debt, the cost of renewing loans and the buying power of budgets. This is a perspective, not a separate score.

Valuation methodology and source status

The traditional US Buffett Indicator divides nonfinancial corporate equities at market value (Federal Reserve Z.1 LM103164105.Q, corresponding to FRED NCBEILQ027S) by trailing four-quarter US nominal GDP (FU086902005.Q). The meter ranks the latest observation against earlier annual Q4 observations in a trailing thirty-calendar-year window, excluding the latest year. At least ten earlier observations are required. Percentile = (lower values + half of equal values) / comparison count × 100. The descriptive bands (low at or below twenty; elevated at or above eighty) are editorial conventions, not validated fair-value thresholds. Missing quarters are not interpolated.

How to read this page

Economic figures are checked when the dashboard refreshes, but the latest published readings may be older. Regional figures show the middle value for selected countries, not all countries in the region.

The imaging commentary was reviewed on 17 October 2026. US borrowing, medical-care CPI, OECD scanner statistics and Federal Reserve Z.1 Buffett Indicator series carry their own publication dates. Historical comparisons are not predictions.

Detailed methods, limitations and data sources

Regional economic growth and inflation show unweighted medians across the listed country sample and preserve missing or unverified observations rather than substituting estimates. US federal funds and medical-care CPI series come from official Federal Reserve and BLS releases via FRED; US market valuation compares Federal Reserve Z.1 nonfinancial corporate equities with trailing four-quarter nominal GDP; imaging utilisation and scanner density come from OECD Health Statistics.

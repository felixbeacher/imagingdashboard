CT: capacity, use and innovation

Aim of this page

To understand CT as a clinical imaging modality and an equipment market: track scanner availability and examination activity, compare regional reporting, follow technology developments and research, and assess competitive industry data using original sources and clearly identified assumptions.

The selected region controls OECD CT equipment and examination data only. Market values, manufacturer shares and the research feed remain global. Country observations are selected samples, not regional averages.

Source definitions, dates and estimate limitations

The market-size figures are published by independent commercial research firms; their segment boundaries, reporting periods and proprietary calculations are not necessarily identical. Their variation is a publisher comparison, not a statistically calculated confidence interval. Forecasts have not been mixed with historical estimates.

The DBS market-share table is sourced from its August 2025 report (page 29) and gives no clear measurement basis or source observation year. “Other manufacturers” is calculated as the unlisted remainder to 100%. Neither table can establish individual CT sales revenue, scanner placements or geographic market shares.

Source checks run on publication builds; a source that cannot be accessed continues to show its original publication date and is not relabelled as new market data. CT research and OECD data are collected separately.

Where CT fits in to clinical care

A concise clinical orientation, not a recommendation for an individual patient. Indications and technique depend on appropriateness criteria, radiation considerations and local protocols.

Emergency and trauma

What it measures: CT depicts acute injuries, haemorrhage and relevant anatomy rapidly.

Why it matters for imaging: emergency access depends on scanner availability, staffing, protocols and reporting capacity.

Oncology

What it measures: cross-sectional anatomy and change in tumour burden over time.

Why it matters for imaging: repeat examinations and protocol consistency can influence service demand.

Cardiac and vascular CT

What it measures: contrast-enhanced vascular anatomy and selected coronary features.

Why it matters for imaging: application requires appropriate acquisition, contrast management and expertise.

Chest and lung assessment

What it measures: pulmonary anatomy, focal abnormalities and interstitial patterns.

Why it matters for imaging: protocols and screening suitability vary by clinical question.

Interventional and planning CT

What it measures: anatomy used in image guidance and pre-procedure planning.

Why it matters for imaging: workflow, CT room access and multidisciplinary coordination affect service delivery.

Paediatric CT

What it measures: anatomy where CT is justified for a specific paediatric question.

Why it matters for imaging: age-appropriate protocols, consideration of alternatives and optimisation are particularly important.

Clinical appropriateness reference: ACR Appropriateness Criteria. Definitions are general reference material; the research cards above are live observations.

Sources, coverage and methodology

Detailed interpretation and update rules
  • OECD capacity is CT scanners per million people. Examination activity is CT examinations per 1,000 people. Source definitions and coverage can vary by country and provider.
  • Each country in the selected region has its own colour. Published observations are shown as solid lines; sufficiently supported modelled extensions are dashed in the same colour, with sensitivity shading. Gaps remain gaps, and no regional mean or total is calculated.
  • CT projections require four consecutive annual source observations ending in 2024 or later. A model fitted to the first three predicts the fourth as a one-year historical holdout; forecasts are withheld if the relative error exceeds 10% for scanner density or 15% for examinations. If the holdout is adequate, an annualised trend is fitted on all four readings, capped at ±5% and ±8% respectively. The widening ±2-percentage-point annual-growth sensitivity is not a statistical confidence interval, and the result is not an OECD forecast. Actual published readings always take precedence over modelled ones.
  • PubMed searches CT-specific titles from the preceding 365 days and evaluates a recent candidate pool; case reports, editorials, reviews and non-X-ray tomography are excluded. Verified indexed publication types and specialist-journal relevance inform an editorial prioritisation that limits repeated topics. Displayed papers are a selected discovery sample, not a systematic review, validated quality ranking or measure of clinical benefit.
  • Data are retrieved at automated rebuild time; "retrieved" is not the date that a study or annual CT observation was recorded. Any unavailable source is labelled as such.

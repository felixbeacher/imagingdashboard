Randomised trial · 24 March 2026

LungIMPACT: AI prioritisation in the lung cancer pathway →

A prospective multicentre trial analysed 93,326 chest X-rays. AI worklist prioritisation did not significantly shorten time to CT or lung cancer diagnosis. This evaluates a particular pathway intervention; it does not settle every use of chest X-ray AI.

Prospective study · 2026

AI assistance for chest radiograph interpretation →

A multicentre prospective evaluation included deployment in 296 patients. Report quality and reading efficiency were studied with radiologist review. These workflow findings require validation across other settings and patient groups.

The Ark+ work examines transferable representations for chest radiographs. Benchmark performance supports further development but does not establish improved patient outcomes in routine deployment.

Where X-ray fits into clinical care

Chest radiography

Frequently used in chest assessment and to check lines or devices. A two-dimensional projection can conceal overlapping abnormalities; further imaging depends on the clinical question.

Bones and joints

Commonly used to assess fractures, alignment and degenerative change. Some occult injuries and soft-tissue questions require a different examination.

Mobile and bedside imaging

Brings radiography to patients who are difficult to move. Positioning, infection control and image quality remain important service considerations.

Tuberculosis screening

Chest radiography, sometimes supported by CAD, can identify people needing further TB investigation. An abnormality score alone does not confirm TB.

WHO screening guidance →

Related X-ray services

Fluoroscopy provides moving images; mammography and dental radiography have dedicated equipment and pathways. Their volumes are not merged into the plain-radiography sample.

Justification and optimisation

X-rays use ionising radiation. Services must justify examinations and optimise exposure for the clinical task, including appropriate paediatric protocols.

Technology shaping X-ray services

Digital detectors

Flat-panel systems support digital acquisition and image distribution. Detector durability, calibration, replacement cost and interoperability matter alongside image quality.

Positioning and automation

Motorised movement and positioning assistance can support repeatable acquisitions. Evaluate them against local workflow, accessibility, training and repeat-image rates.

Mobile radiography

Portable equipment supports bedside and outreach services. Battery life, manoeuvrability, servicing and reliable transfer into PACS affect usefulness.

Dual-energy and spectral methods

Energy-dependent attenuation can help separate materials. Availability and evidence are task-specific; do not transfer CT results automatically to projection radiography.

Technical review · 2021 →

AI and reporting

Detection, worklist triage and report drafting address different tasks. Diagnostic accuracy, workflow benefit and patient outcomes need separate evaluation.

Quality assurance

Monitor exposure indices, rejected images and dose trends. Improved post-processing does not remove the need to control acquisition exposure.

Detailed interpretation and update rules
  • The region selector changes regional activity coverage and guidance only. It does not relabel global market data or research as regional.
  • The England source is a national sample within Europe. Neither its activity nor its growth rate is extrapolated to other countries.
  • No interpolation, regional mean, per-capita conversion or forecast is calculated. Missing observations remain unavailable.
  • Clinical uses and technologies are background context. Manufacturer descriptions are product information rather than independent performance evidence.
  • This page is a dated source snapshot. The source links lead to current publications, but opening the page does not refresh its figures. Selected papers include older foundational context and are not restricted to the last year.

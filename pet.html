Where PET fits into clinical care

PET maps radiotracer distribution. PET/CT adds CT localisation and attenuation correction; PET/MRI combines PET with MR information. Tracer choice determines the biological question, and uptake is not itself a diagnosis.

FDG oncology

Glucose-metabolism imaging supports staging and treatment-response assessment in selected cancers. Inflammation and physiological uptake can complicate interpretation.

Prostate cancer

PSMA-targeted PET supports selected staging and recurrence pathways. Eligibility, interpretation and treatment decisions depend on guideline-defined clinical context.

Neuroendocrine tumours

Somatostatin-receptor PET characterises receptor expression and can support selection for receptor-targeted therapy within specialist pathways.

Brain and neurological disease

FDG, amyloid and tau tracers address different questions. Imaging needs specialist interpretation alongside clinical assessment and local eligibility rules.

Cardiac PET

Tracer-specific protocols evaluate myocardial perfusion, metabolism or inflammation. Stress services, tracer access and quantitative expertise affect capacity.

Infection and inflammation

FDG can support selected specialist investigations. Preparation, timing and clinical context are essential because increased uptake is non-specific.

Clinical references: EANM oncology procedure guidelines and joint EANM/SNMMI PSMA PET/CT guideline. Follow indication-specific standards and local authorisations.

Technology and service considerations

Technology developments to track

Digital detectors and time of flightDetector timing and sensitivity affect image quality and protocol choices. Compare validated task-specific performance rather than a single headline specification.

Long axial field of viewExtended coverage collects more simultaneous information and enables dynamic research. The balance between acquisition time, activity and image quality remains protocol-dependent.

Reconstruction and quantificationAI reconstruction, motion correction and harmonised calibration require clinical validation. Consistent preparation, uptake timing and reconstruction matter for longitudinal measurements.

Tracer supply and operational capacityRadioactive decay, production slots, transport, uptake rooms, radiation protection and staffing can constrain a service even when scanner time is available.

Examples: Siemens long-axial-field PET/CT; United Imaging total-body PET/CT. Manufacturer descriptions, not independent comparative evidence.

Sources, coverage and methodology

Detailed interpretation and update rules
  • OECD capacity is PET scanners per million people. Examination activity is PET scans per 1,000 people per year. Source definitions and coverage can vary by country and provider.
  • Each country in the selected region has its own colour. Published observations are shown as solid lines; sufficiently supported modelled extensions are dashed in the same colour, with sensitivity shading. Gaps remain gaps, and no regional mean or total is calculated.
  • PET projections require four consecutive annual source observations ending in 2024 or later. A model fitted to the first three predicts the fourth as a one-year historical holdout; forecasts are withheld if the relative error exceeds 10% for scanner density or 15% for examinations. If the holdout is adequate, an annualised trend is fitted on all four readings, capped at ±5% and ±8% respectively. The widening ±2-percentage-point annual-growth sensitivity is not a statistical confidence interval, and the result is not an OECD forecast. Actual published readings always take precedence over modelled ones.
  • PubMed searches PET-specific titles from the preceding 365 days and evaluates a recent candidate pool; case reports, editorials, reviews and non-PET articles are excluded. Verified indexed publication types and specialist-journal relevance inform an editorial prioritisation that limits repeated topics. Displayed papers are a selected discovery sample, not a systematic review, validated quality ranking or measure of clinical benefit.
  • Data are retrieved at automated rebuild time; "retrieved" is not the date that a study or annual PET observation was recorded. Any unavailable source is labelled as such.

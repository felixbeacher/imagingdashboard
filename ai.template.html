From regulatory authorisation to clinical impact

Adoption means integrating AI into clinical work, not simply obtaining regulatory authorisation or registering a study. Reports should distinguish paid routine use, research pilots and shadow-mode testing, where AI outputs do not guide care. This dashboard does not currently have a validated, comparable global adoption-rate dataset.

What determines successful deployment? Compatibility with imaging systems, local performance testing, staff training, clear clinical responsibility and continued monitoring. Institutional resources and patient populations differ, so implementation findings should be interpreted in their reported setting.

Qualitative context: RSNA implementation and adoption recommendations, 23 February 2026. This dated guidance is separate from the automatically refreshed research below.

01

Regulatory authorisation

Whether a product is authorised for a specific intended use; not proof of routine use.

02

Clinical validation

Performance, generalisability and prospective evaluation in the intended clinical population.

03

Hospital implementation

Integration, workflow acceptance, safety monitoring and sustained routine deployment.

04

Demonstrated benefit

Measured improvements in patient outcomes, quality, timeliness or resources.

No verified, comparable worldwide routine-deployment rate is available. This is a conceptual evidence pathway, not a measured funnel or conversion rate.

Global pressure composite

Five complementary indicators track geopolitical tension, financial stress, supply chains, energy and food prices. The composite is an equal-weight average of historical percentile scores—not a crisis probability or a forecast.

What it measures: how unusually high these pressures are compared with their own past. Why it matters for imaging: global disruptions can affect equipment deliveries, hospital costs and access to finance. Each score runs from 0 to 100. It is not the chance of a future crisis.

Exact monthly scores and component definitions

Geopolitical tension is measured using news reports. Financial stress reflects turbulence in markets. Supply-chain pressure captures shipping and production difficulties. Energy and food measures follow world commodity prices in US dollars without adjusting for inflation. Higher scores mean more pressure relative to each measure’s past.

From macro signals to imaging decisions

Here is how the economic picture could affect choices about imaging equipment and services. These are questions to investigate, not proven predictions.

Purchasing budgets

When the economy slows or borrowing gets expensive, hospitals may have less money for new equipment. Check the approved budget, the cost of buying versus leasing, how urgently a scanner needs replacing and ongoing service charges.

Equipment and delivery

Trade disputes and shipping delays can slow deliveries of scanners, spare parts and repairs. Check delivery times, whether parts may be hard to find, how exchange rates affect the price and what maintenance is included. Compare the full cost over the equipment’s lifetime.

Operating costs

Rising electricity bills, staff costs and service fees can leave less money for imaging. Check local expenses and whether promised savings from faster workflows have actually been shown in a similar clinical setting.

How to read this page

Economic figures are checked when the dashboard refreshes, but the latest published readings may be older. The risk chart is a fixed historical snapshot. Regional figures show the middle value for selected countries, not all countries in the region.

The imaging commentary was reviewed on 17 October 2026. Share-market figures have their own dates. The market-value-to-GDP comparison is annual, and global fund figures use the dates supplied by the data provider. Historical comparisons are not predictions.

Detailed methods, limitations and data sources

Each risk component is scored against a fixed January 2000–December 2025 reference of 312 monthly observations: 100 × (observations below the current value + half the ties) / 312. The composite averages the five scores; it is not itself a historical percentile. OFR daily observations are averaged by month. The supplied chart data have complete monthly coverage, with no interpolation or forward filling.

The scores use past observations; they have not been proven to predict crises. Energy and food prices are not adjusted for inflation. Giving all five measures the same weight is simple but untested. Some may measure similar problems, while others may hide a crisis affecting one region. Climate events, cyberattacks, government debt problems and currency instability are not measured separately.

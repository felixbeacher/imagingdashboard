Published benchmarks · United States

Financing & healthcare cost pressure

Two distinct signals: the overnight financing environment and prices paid for medical care. These US benchmarks retain their own geography when you change economic coverage above.

A policy-linked overnight rate excludes a borrower's credit spread, loan term and fees. Medical-care consumer prices do not measure hospital wages, equipment purchase costs or the full cost of providing care.

UK context and source routes

For UK decisions, check Bank of England Bank Rate, ONS consumer-price data and the provider's actual financing terms. UK rates and inflation are not automatically collected here; do not substitute the US figures.

Analytical guide · FTSE 100 as a UK example

Valuation: opportunity and financial resilience

Examine both the price paid and the resilience of the underlying cash flows. The compact FTSE figures below reproduce the supplied page’s illustrative series; they are separate from the refreshed economic data.

The FTSE 100 is a broad UK-listed equity index, not a medical-imaging index or a direct measure of hospital demand. Start with its official index definition and factsheet before drawing comparisons.

Which valuation measures are useful—and where do they mislead?
Definitions and analytical limitations. No current multiples are asserted.
MeasureUseful questionLimit to check
P/E & CAPEWhat price is paid for current earnings or a longer earnings history?Trailing and forecast P/E differ. CAPE smooths inflation-adjusted earnings over a long window; sector mix, accounting and business changes still matter.
P/B & P/SHow does equity value compare with book equity or sales?Book value can miss internally developed intangible assets. Sales multiples ignore profitability; neither measure removes leverage risk.
Enterprise value & cash flowHow does valuation look after accounting for debt, cash and reinvestment?Use consistent definitions. EBITDA excludes costs that matter, including capital expenditure; recurring service cash flow deserves separate scrutiny.
Dividend yield & inflationHow large is the cash distribution relative to price pressure?Dividend yield minus CPI is only a rough spread, not an investor's real total return. Price changes, reinvestment, tax and dividend cuts matter.
Equities relative to goldHow has one asset performed relative to another?Use consistent currency and total-return treatment. A price-index/gold-price ratio is not a CPI-adjusted return or a literal holding of gold ounces.
Market capitalisation / GDPHow large is a defined equity market relative to a defined economy?The numerator's coverage must be stated. FTSE 100 capitalisation is not the whole UK equity market, and company revenue exposure need not match domestic GDP.

From macro signals to imaging decisions

Use these questions to connect the context above to an actual procurement or investment decision. These are analytical considerations, not measured effects.

Purchasing budgets

Slower growth and tighter financing can constrain capital spending. Check approved capital budgets, lease-versus-buy terms, replacement urgency and service costs. A market valuation discount is not evidence that a provider can afford a scanner.

Equipment and delivery

Trade disruption and supply-chain pressure can affect components, shipping and service availability. Check quoted lead times, component exposure, currency clauses and service coverage. Compare whole-life cost rather than the headline purchase price alone.

Operating costs

Energy and inflation pressures can squeeze operating budgets. Consider local electricity prices, staffing, service contracts and currency movements; ask whether expected workflow savings have been demonstrated in the intended care setting.

Method, limitations and attribution

Each risk component is scored against a fixed January 2000–December 2025 reference of 312 monthly observations: 100 × (observations below the current value + half the ties) / 312. The composite averages the five scores; it is not itself a historical percentile. OFR daily observations are averaged by month. The supplied chart data have complete monthly coverage, with no interpolation or forward filling.

The calibration is retrospective, so this is not a real-time backtest. Energy and food indices are nominal, not inflation-adjusted. Equal weights are transparent but not empirically validated: correlated components can duplicate shocks, while calm components can dilute a regional crisis. Climate, cyber incidents, sovereign-default risk and currency instability have no dedicated components.
